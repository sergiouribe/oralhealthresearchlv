§ 01 — Thesis/ i

Why children's teeth are a public‑health leading indicator.

Children's oral health reveals a nation's future.

Maximum impact.
Minimum cost.

There is no health
without oral health.
— WHO, 2024

Caries today predicts diabetes tomorrow and cardiovascular disease in twenty years. This progression is preventable at the oral health stage — the moment of maximum impact and minimum cost.

Countries prioritising children's dental care gain immediate returns: children miss fewer school days and learn more effectively, while parents avoid the 3.5 workdays annually lost to managing dental emergencies. Long‑term savings follow through lower chronic disease burden and healthier populations.

Countries with lower caries prevalence show higher GDP, better educational outcomes, and lower chronic disease burden. Oral health predicts prosperity.

A population of 1.9 million makes Latvia uniquely suited to rapid national‑scale implementation. Evidence‑based interventions and new technologies like Artificial Intelligence can be evaluated at population scale, then adapted for international contexts.

We work to close this gap — equipping clinicians with evidence of high‑value healthcare, identifying low‑value care for de‑implementation, providing policymakers with cost‑effectiveness data, and deploying technology to reduce the implementation gap.

Latvian National Guideline for Fluoride Use

Establishes national standards for caries prevention — water fluoridation, topical fluoride products, school programs. Co‑authored by Profs. Maldupa & Uribe; endorsed by the Latvian Dental Association.

LZA · Ministry of Health
2023Survey

National Oral Health Survey · 3,943 adolescents

Only 14% of 12‑year‑olds caries‑free. D3MFT 2.3 at age 12, 4.4 at age 15 — exceeding European averages. Strong associations with inadequate fluoride toothpaste use, rural residence, and daily sugary drink consumption.

SPKC 2022–2023Baseline for policy dev.

Child Dental Neglect — Legal Protections Compendium

Compiles oral health profiles across 26 countries and Hong Kong SAR. 25/26 have general neglect laws; only 2 have dental‑specific. Latvia & Chile documentation contributed by Maldupa & Uribe.
